Is R10,000 a Month Enough for Rental Arbitrage in Johannesburg?

September 2026By the Luxenest Design Team6 min read

Yes, R10,000 a month can work for rental arbitrage in Johannesburg. But rent is only one of five numbers that decide whether a unit makes money, and it is not the one that surprises people most.

When a new client tells us they have “around R10,000 a month for rent”, our first question is not about the apartment. It is about the rest of the budget. Here is how we think it through.

What you pay every month

Rent is the biggest fixed cost, but it comes with company: utilities, Wi-Fi, cleaning and laundry for every stay, and platform fees. If you use a management service, add its fee. In our example, a one-bedroom with R10,000 rent carries about R11,500 of fixed costs each month before a single guest arrives (rent plus about R1,500 for utilities and Wi-Fi).

Where the break-even sits

Assume a nightly rate of R1,100, cleaning of R350 per stay with an average stay of three nights, and a 3% platform fee. Each booked night then leaves about R950. Divide the R11,500 of fixed costs by that and you need roughly 12 booked nights a month, or about 40% occupancy, just to break even. Every night beyond that is profit. Your real numbers will depend on the building, the area and how the listing performs.

The upfront number people forget

A monthly budget of R10,000 does not cover the cost of getting started. For a one-bedroom, plan for:

  • Furnishing and setup: about R63,500An Airbnb-ready one-bedroom with Luxenest: furnishing package, R10,000 setup and sourcing fee, bedding and bathroom essentials, incl. VAT.
  • Deposit: often one to two months' rentThat is R10,000 to R20,000 at this rent. Ask whether the landlord charges more for short-term use.
  • A two-month reserve: about R23,000New listings need a few weeks to collect their first reviews. A reserve covers rent and utilities while bookings build.

Together that is roughly R95,000 to R110,000 to start comfortably. If that is more than you have available, you can phase the setup: furnish the essentials first (bed, sofa, kitchen basics, linen and bathroom), then add décor upgrades once bookings come in.

“The real question is not whether you can afford the rent. It is whether you can afford the slow first month.”

If the numbers look tight

  • Compare a slightly higher rent in a stronger locationA unit that books at a higher rate and occupancy can beat a cheaper one that sits empty.
  • Consider a studio or a smaller footprintLower rent and a lower furnishing cost improve your break-even point.
  • Get the rate assumption checkedNightly rate and occupancy are the biggest swing factors. We check comparable listings before you sign.
  • Walk away if it only works at 75% occupancyIf a unit is profitable only in the strong scenario, the risk is too high.
